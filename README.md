## Overview

The Gettysburg Battlefield, in Adams County, Pennsylvania, is the site of the three-day Battle of Gettysburg (July 1–3, 1863), the deadliest battle of the American Civil War. Combined casualty estimates range from 46,000 to 51,000 killed, wounded, captured, or missing. Confederate General Robert E. Lee's Army of Northern Virginia was repulsed by Union Major General George G. Meade's Army of the Potomac, and the battle is widely regarded as the war's turning point. Lincoln's Gettysburg Address, delivered at the dedication of the Soldiers' National Cemetery on November 19, 1863, secured the battlefield's place in American memory. The site became Gettysburg National Military Park in 1895, and is now operated by the National Park Service. Alongside its historical significance, Gettysburg is one of the most-cited haunted locations in the United States. Ghost stories are documented from the immediate post-battle period, when townspeople and burial details described unusual sounds and apparitions, and have continued to multiply since. Specific paranormal traditions cluster at Devil's Den, the Triangular Field, Little Round Top, the Wheatfield, Sachs Bridge, the Jennie Wade House (where the only documented civilian killed in the battle, Mary Virginia 'Jennie' Wade, was struck by a stray bullet on July 3, 1863), the Daniel Lady Farm, and the Farnsworth House Inn. Mark Nesbitt, a former park ranger, published the first volume of his 'Ghosts of Gettysburg' series in 1991 and founded the Ghosts of Gettysburg Candlelight Walking Tours in 1994, helping anchor the modern ghost-tourism industry. Mainstream historians and the National Park Service treat the ghost stories as folklore.

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Residual haunting (stone-tape) of mass trauma** _(Mark Nesbitt, various paranormal investigators)_

The intensity of casualties and emotion at Gettysburg is hypothesized to have 'imprinted' on the landscape, producing repeating, non-interactive playbacks of battle scenes (apparitions, sounds).

**Intelligent hauntings of the unfinished dead** _(Mark Nesbitt, Ghosts of Gettysburg tour guides)_

Some accounts describe interactive apparitions (e.g. the mounted rider directing the 20th Maine, or Jennie Wade's reported activity in her house) interpreted as conscious spirits.

### Skeptical

**Folklore and ghost-tourism amplification** _(Joe Nickell, Benjamin Radford)_

Ghost reports cluster around well-known landmarks featured in commercial tours and books; the modern reputation is largely the product of post-1991 ghost-tourism rather than a continuous tradition.

**Misperception, suggestion, and equipment artefacts** _(Joe Nickell, Brian Dunning)_

Visual and auditory misperception in low light, expectation effects from tour narration, infrasound from terrain, and ordinary equipment malfunctions account for most reports.
